Brain Teams

What it is. Brain regions work in networks connected by long fibre tracts. These networks remain coordinated even at rest.

What it gives you. This shows how regions combine their work, and identifies the network that supports general problem-solving and fluid intelligence.

Regions work as networks

Specialised regions do not work alone. Long fibre tracts link them into networks, and regions within a network rise and fall in activity together, even when a person is simply resting in a scanner.

Three networks are especially clear. The default mode network is active during mind-wandering. The multiple-demand network engages for any hard task. The language network stays distinct from both.

The multiple-demand network appears to be the brain's general-purpose problem solver. Damage to it lowers fluid intelligence in proportion to the tissue lost, while similar damage elsewhere does not.

The wiring

  • 45% of the human brain is white matter (about 10% in mice)
  • 3x around the Earth, if all fibres were laid end to end
  • 10% of white matter fibres lost per decade after age 20

Three systems

  • Default mode Active when no external task is given: memory, planning and social thought.
  • Multiple demand Engages for any difficult task: working memory, arithmetic, fine discrimination.
  • Language Its regions fluctuate together even 10 to 12 cm apart, and separately from the multiple-demand network.

Wiring determines function

  1. The inputs a region receives determine what information it can represent.
  2. Its outputs determine what it can influence.
  3. Children with dyslexia show a less organised tract between two language regions.
  4. Brains minimise wiring length, like a well-designed circuit board, because fibres cost space and metabolic fuel.
  5. Damage to the multiple-demand network lowers IQ in proportion to tissue lost. Damage elsewhere does not.

The multiple-demand network is the brain's general-purpose problem solver.

Resting-state scans require no task at all. Volunteers often fall asleep while the scanner maps their networks.

Steps

  1. Lie down for five minutes with your eyes closed and no task.
  2. Notice where your thoughts go.
  3. Memories, plans and other people. This is the default network at work.
  4. Then calculate 17 × 13 in your head.
  5. Notice the shift. The multiple-demand network takes over for effortful tasks.

Time. 7 minutes

Words to know

White matter
Long nerve fibres wrapped in myelin, connecting distant brain regions.
Default mode network
Regions most active during rest, mind-wandering and thinking about oneself or others.
Multiple-demand network
Frontal and parietal regions that activate for any difficult cognitive task.
