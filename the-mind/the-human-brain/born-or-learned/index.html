Born or Learned?

What it is. Some face-processing abilities are present within hours of birth. Experience then refines them and narrows them to the faces we actually encounter.

What it gives you. This shows nature and nurture working together rather than in opposition, and explains why early experience has such lasting effects.

A fast start, then refinement

Within an hour of birth, infants track a simple face-like pattern further than a scrambled version. Within days they can tell two unfamiliar faces apart. Some sensitivity to faces appears to be built in.

Early abilities are also broad. At six months, infants distinguish monkey faces nearly as well as human ones, and hear speech sounds from every language. By nine to twelve months, both abilities narrow to the faces and sounds they meet daily.

This narrowing is not damage. It is efficient specialisation. The same process explains why people often find faces from unfamiliar groups harder to tell apart, an effect that is largely reversible with exposure in childhood.

A rapid timeline

  • 1 hour old: newborns track a face pattern further than a scrambled one
  • 1-3 days old: infants distinguish two new faces
  • 6 months infants distinguish individual monkey faces
  • 9 months that ability has largely disappeared
  • 4 years adult-like face skills are in place
  • 2x brain size increase in the first year

The habituation method

  1. Show an infant the same image repeatedly.
  2. Looking time falls with each repetition as interest fades.
  3. Then present a new image.
  4. If looking time rises again, the infant perceives the new image as different.
  5. This lets researchers ask what infants can distinguish before they can speak.

Infants cannot answer questions, but their eyes can.

Abilities that narrow

  • Faces of other species Distinguishable at six months, largely lost by nine.
  • Foreign speech sounds Infants hear contrasts from every language. Adults hear mainly those of their own.
  • Preserving the ability Labelling individual monkey faces by name between 6 and 12 months maintains the skill. General exposure does not.

The same applies to faces of other ethnic groups. Moving to a new country before about age 12 typically reverses it.

Monkeys raised by caretakers wearing masks never saw a face during infancy. When they first did, they still preferred faces and could tell them apart.

Steps

  1. Find photos of two monkeys and two human strangers.
  2. Show someone one monkey face, then both, and ask which they saw first.
  3. Repeat with the two human faces.
  4. Humans are easy; monkeys are hard. At six months of age, the same person could tell monkey faces apart as well.

Time. 5 minutes

Words to know

Habituation
A decline in looking or response to a repeated stimulus. Renewed interest in a new stimulus shows it is perceived as different.
Perceptual narrowing
The loss, during the first year, of the ability to discriminate stimuli that are not part of everyday experience.
Innate
Present from birth, without needing to be learned.
