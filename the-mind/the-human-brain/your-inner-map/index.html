Your Inner Map

What it is. Specialised neurons in and around the hippocampus encode where you are, which way you face and how far you have travelled.

What it gives you. These cells show how the brain maintains a live map of space, why that map sometimes flips, and how the same machinery may organise abstract thought.

A map made of neurons

In the 1970s, recordings from the rat hippocampus revealed place cells: neurons that fire only when the animal is in one particular spot. Each cell has its own spot, and together they tile the whole environment.

Later work found head-direction cells, which act as a compass, and grid cells, which fire in a hexagonal lattice across the floor and measure distance. Border cells mark walls and edges. The discoverers of place and grid cells shared the Nobel Prize in 2014.

This system is not limited to physical space. Similar grid-like patterns appear when people learn abstract relationships, suggesting that the brain uses spatial machinery to organise many kinds of information.

Three cell types

  • Place cells Each fires in one location, regardless of which way the animal faces.
  • Head-direction cells Each fires when the head points one way. Together they form a compass.
  • Grid cells Fire at the vertices of a hexagonal grid across the environment, providing a measure of distance.

Border cells add walls and drop-offs. The 2014 Nobel Prize recognised place and grid cells.

Geometry outweighs colour

  1. A rat sees food hidden in one corner of a rectangular room, then is disoriented.
  2. It searches the correct corner and the diagonally opposite one equally often. Both have the same geometry.
  3. One distinctly coloured wall would resolve the ambiguity. The rat ignores it.
  4. Toddlers make the same error, even when one wall is bright red and plays music.
  5. Adults do too, when their verbal system is occupied by repeating speech aloud.

Colours change with the seasons; the shape of the landscape does not.

A map for thinking

  • Abstract dimensions Learning imaginary birds that vary in neck and leg length produces a grid-like signal.
  • Social space Status and closeness can be represented like coordinates. Bats track the position of other bats.
  • Planning At a junction, a rat's place cells briefly replay both possible paths before it chooses.

The sudden sense of facing the wrong way in a familiar place has a neural basis: the whole population of place cells can remap at once.

Safety. Stop if you feel dizzy; keep a friend beside you while your eyes are closed.

Steps

  1. Sit in a swivel chair with your eyes closed while someone turns you slowly, then stops.
  2. Point to the door.
  3. Your internal compass still has an estimate, updated by the vestibular system in your inner ear.
  4. With eyes open, walk ten steps, turn left and walk five more.
  5. Without looking, point back to your starting position.
  6. Direction and distance signals have integrated your path, much like the desert ant.

Time. 5 minutes

Words to know

Place cell
A hippocampal neuron that fires when the animal occupies one specific location.
Grid cell
A neuron in entorhinal cortex that fires at many locations arranged in a hexagonal pattern, providing a metric for distance.
Head-direction cell
A neuron that fires when the head points in one particular direction, acting as an internal compass.
