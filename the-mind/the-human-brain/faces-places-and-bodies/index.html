Faces, Places and Bodies

What it is. Only a handful of categories have their own dedicated region in virtually every human brain: faces, places, bodies and, in readers, written words.

What it gives you. This shows which kinds of visual information the brain treats as biologically special, and raises the question of how far brain scans can read the mind.

Which categories get a region?

Scanning studies have tested many categories of image to find which ones have their own dedicated region. The list is short. Faces, places, bodies and written words each have one in almost everyone.

Many other categories that matter for survival, such as snakes, spiders, food and tools, show no such dedicated area. They are processed by more general systems spread across the ventral visual cortex.

Pattern analysis adds a further layer. Even regions with weak responses can carry information in their pattern of activity. Whether that information is actually used by the brain is a separate question.

Around 20 categories compared

  • Faces A dedicated region in virtually everyone.
  • Places and scenes A dedicated region in virtually everyone.
  • Bodies A dedicated region in virtually everyone.
  • Written words A dedicated region, but only after learning to read.
  • Snakes and spiders No dedicated region found.
  • Food, flowers, cars, chairs No dedicated region found.

Does the face area also know about chairs?

  • The challenge Even a weak response can carry a pattern that distinguishes chairs from cars.
  • The finding Pattern analysis shows that it does, to a small degree.
  • The reply A vending machine can be used to tell the time from its display. That is not its function.

Can a scan read your mind?

  1. Scan a person viewing shoes, then cats, and store each activity pattern.
  2. Show a new image and ask which stored pattern it resembles more.
  3. Accuracy is above chance, but modest.
  4. It is easily defeated: small head or tongue movements, or mental arithmetic, disrupt it.
  5. In a monkey, 167 individual neurons distinguished five faces. An fMRI scan of the same region could not.

Each fMRI voxel averages the activity of hundreds of thousands of neurons.

When people attend to letters flashing over a face, rather than the face itself, the face area responds less. Attention changes how strongly the brain processes what the eyes receive.

Steps

  1. Find a busy photograph, such as a crowd or a street scene.
  2. Look at it for three seconds, then close your eyes.
  3. Describe everything you remember.
  4. People, faces and the layout of the place come first. Each has a dedicated system.
  5. Now name three objects from the photo.
  6. Objects come more slowly. No single region handles cars or chairs; they are processed more diffusely.

Time. 3 minutes

Words to know

Category selectivity
A region responding much more strongly to one kind of stimulus, such as faces, than to others.
MVPA
Multivoxel pattern analysis: reading which stimulus a person saw from the pattern of activity across many voxels.
Fusiform face area
A region on the underside of the temporal lobe that responds most strongly to faces.
