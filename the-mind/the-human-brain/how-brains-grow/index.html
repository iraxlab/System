How Brains Grow

What it is. What a brain region becomes depends heavily on the input it receives and how it is connected to the rest of the brain.

What it gives you. This explains what experience can reshape, what stays fixed, and why recovery from brain injury depends so much on age.

Input and wiring shape function

A brain region is not destined for one role from the start. Its function depends on what information reaches it and where its outputs go. Change the input early enough, and the region can take on a very different job.

Reading proves this in humans. Writing was invented only a few thousand years ago, far too recently for evolution to build a dedicated region. Yet every literate adult develops one, in nearly the same location, as they learn to read.

Flexibility declines with age. Damage early in life is often followed by remarkable recovery, as other regions take over. Similar damage in adulthood usually leaves lasting deficits.

Rewiring vision into hearing cortex

  1. Ferrets are born at an early stage, with their eyes still closed.
  2. At birth, input from the eyes was surgically redirected to the auditory cortex.
  3. The auditory cortex developed a layout of orientation-tuned columns typical of visual cortex.
  4. Trained to press one lever for light and another for sound, the animals pressed the light lever when their rewired cortex was stimulated.
  5. They experienced the input as vision.

The input a region receives shapes what it becomes.

The reading region

  • Too recent to be innate Writing is only a few thousand years old, too new for a genetically built region.
  • Acquired at school In the same children, it is absent at age 5 and present by age 8.
  • Script-specific It responds to Hebrew only in people who read Hebrew.

Its future location can be predicted from a child's connectivity at age 5, before reading begins.

What recovers and what does not

  • Adult stroke Language rarely returns fully after damage to the left-hemisphere language areas.
  • Damage in infancy Language often shifts to the right hemisphere and develops well.
  • Stroke at birth One boy developed normal object recognition but never learned to recognise faces.
  • Congenital blindness Visual cortex takes on roles in language and other non-visual tasks.
  • Blind from birth Face-related sounds, such as laughter and chewing, still activate the usual face region.
  • Brain-training apps Performance improves on the trained game, but rarely transfers to general ability.

Because nerve fibres are wrapped in fat, water moves along them more easily than across them. Diffusion MRI follows this water to reconstruct the brain's wiring.

Steps

  1. Choose a script you cannot read, such as Hebrew, Chinese or Arabic.
  2. Study one line for ten seconds, then copy it from memory.
  3. Do the same with a line in English.
  4. English is reproduced as whole units; the unfamiliar script comes out in fragments. Your reading region is tuned to your own writing system, learned only after school began.

Time. 5 minutes

Words to know

Diffusion imaging
An MRI method that traces fibre bundles by following the direction in which water moves along them.
Visual word form area
A region on the left underside of the brain that becomes specialised for written words as a person learns to read.
Critical period
An early window when a brain system is especially responsive to experience.
