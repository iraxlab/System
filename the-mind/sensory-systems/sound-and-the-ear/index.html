Sound and the Ear

What it is. Sound is a pressure wave in air. The outer ear collects it and shapes it, the middle ear passes it from air into fluid, and the inner ear sorts it by frequency.

What it gives you. Understanding the ear explains decibels, why some hearing losses can be repaired by surgery, and how the shape of your ear helps you hear height.

From air to fluid

Sound is mechanical vibration carried through air, water or solids. A loudspeaker pushes and pulls the air, making tiny pressure swings above and below normal air pressure. There is no sound in a vacuum.

Getting sound from air into the fluid of the inner ear is hard: about 99.5% of sound bounces off a water surface. The middle ear solves this. A large eardrum collects force onto a small footplate, and the three bones add leverage, raising the pressure.

Without the eardrum and bones, hearing drops by about 40 dB. That is a serious loss, but not deafness, and surgery or a hearing aid can often restore it.

Frequency, level and quality

  • Frequency Vibrations per second, in hertz. Young humans hear about 20 Hz to 20 kHz.
  • Level Measured in decibels. 0 dB is roughly the faintest audible 1 kHz tone.
  • Pitch Where a sound sits on a musical scale, mainly set by its fundamental frequency.
  • Harmonics Instruments vibrate at the fundamental and at its multiples.
  • Timbre The pattern of harmonics, which makes each instrument and voice distinct.
  • Smallest changes About 1 dB in level and about 2 Hz at 1,000 Hz in frequency.

The anatomy of the ear

  • Outer ear The visible ear and a 3 cm canal ending at the eardrum, which is as thin as plastic wrap.
  • Middle ear An air-filled space with the three smallest bones of the body. The stirrup presses on the oval window.
  • Inner ear The cochlea, the size of an aspirin tablet, in the hardest bone of the skull. The balance canals sit beside it.

Everyday decibels

  • 0 dB faintest sound young ears detect
  • 60 dB normal conversation
  • 85 dB level where hearing protection is advised over a working day
  • 100 dB chainsaw or leaf blower
  • 120 dB pain begins; damage after brief exposure
  • 140 dB+ gunshot or jet engine close by

How ear shape gives height

  1. Sound reflects off the folds of the outer ear before entering the canal.
  2. Reflections cancel the direct sound at certain frequencies, cutting sharp notches in the spectrum.
  3. The notches move with the height of the sound source.
  4. In one study, moulds placed in the ears ruined people's judgement of height but not of left and right.
  5. After a few weeks wearing them, people learned their new ears and judged height well again.

Left and right come from comparing the two ears. Height comes from the shape of each ear.

The decibel reference was set in the 1930s by testing the hearing of a few dozen people at a county fair. Young listeners often hear slightly better, so values below 0 dB are possible.

Safety. Keep any test sound at a comfortable level, and stop at once if your ears ring or hurt.

Steps

  1. Sit somewhere quiet with your eyes closed and ask a friend to jingle keys around your head.
  2. Point to where the sound comes from, including high and low positions.
  3. You point well. The folds of the outer ear filter sound differently from each height.
  4. Swallow or yawn while gently pinching your nose, as on a plane.
  5. Your ears pop. A small tube equalises air pressure on both sides of the eardrum.
  6. Hum with your ears blocked by your fingers.
  7. The hum gets louder. Sound reaches the inner ear through the bones of the skull too.

Time. 5 minutes

Words to know

Decibel (dB SPL)
A logarithmic scale of sound pressure. Every 20 dB is a tenfold rise in pressure.
Ossicles
The three small bones of the middle ear: hammer, anvil and stirrup.
Timbre
The quality that makes a piano and a guitar sound different on the same note.
Conductive hearing loss
Loss caused by a problem in the outer or middle ear, before the cochlea.
