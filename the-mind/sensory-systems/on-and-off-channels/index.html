ON and OFF Channels

What it is. The retina splits its output into two parallel channels: ON cells signal places that become brighter than their background, OFF cells signal places that become darker.

What it gives you. Seeing dark print on a light page uses the OFF system, seeing a bright star the ON system. Splitting the two lets the eye report both quickly and with excitation.

Two channels from the first synapse

Receptors release glutamate in the dark and less in light. The bipolar cells that listen to them come in two kinds. OFF bipolars follow the receptor signal. ON bipolars use a special receptor, mGluR6, which inverts it, so they are excited by light.

From there the two channels stay apart. ON and OFF ganglion cells branch in separate sublayers of the retina, and each type tiles the retina evenly on its own, ignoring the other. They are two complete, overlapping systems.

A drug called APB fills the ON receptor and freezes it. It allowed researchers to switch off the ON channel alone, for about 15 minutes, and watch what the rest of the visual system could still do.

What happens when ON is blocked

  1. ON-centre cells in the thalamus stop responding to light. OFF-centre cells carry on as before.
  2. OFF cells keep their full surround, so centre and surround do not come from the ON-OFF interplay.
  3. Cortical cells lose their response to light edges but keep their tuning to angle, direction and stripe width.
  4. In a task, light spots are found barely above chance and slowly; dark spots are found as quickly as ever.
  5. After the drug washes out, every response returns.

The tuning of cortical cells is built by circuits in the cortex itself, not by mixing ON and OFF inputs.

Rods borrow the cone wiring

  • One rod bipolar Rods have only an ON-type bipolar cell. Block it and night vision fails for both light and dark spots.
  • A shared route A special amacrine cell passes the rod signal into the cone ON and OFF pathways, so rods and cones share ganglion cells.
  • Bigger fields at night In the dark, receptive fields widen and colour responses vanish. A red rose looks black by moonlight.

People with congenital night blindness often lack the ON channel and may be limited to daytime driving.

What the split buys

  • Light on dark A predator lit from above looks bright to a fish below. ON cells catch it fast.
  • Dark on light A bird against the sky looks dark. So does print on paper. OFF cells catch these.
  • Speed Both changes are signalled by an increase in firing, which is faster than waiting for a drop.

The ON-OFF test in monkeys

  • 90%+ correct for light and dark spots, normally
  • 250 ms normal time to look at a target
  • 400 ms time to find light spots with ON blocked
  • 250 ms time to find dark spots with ON blocked
  • 2 bipolar cells, at least, for each central cone
  • 15 min for the blocking drug to wash out

Every quick eye movement wipes the retina clean. Transmitter is cleared so fast that the old image is gone within a fraction of a second, ready for the next fixation.

Safety. Never look at the sun or a bright lamp, and stop if your eyes strain or water.

Steps

  1. Look at a page of dark text on a white background.
  2. The letters are darker than their surround. OFF cells carry most of this signal.
  3. Look at a white light on a dark wall at night.
  4. Now ON cells carry the signal. Each channel has its own cells, its own wiring and its own receptor chemistry.
  5. Stare at a bright window for 20 seconds, then look at a plain wall.
  6. A dark afterimage appears. Adapted cells answer the plain wall with the opposite signal.

Time. 3 minutes

Words to know

ON cell
A cell excited when its receptive field becomes brighter than the background.
OFF cell
A cell excited when its receptive field becomes darker than the background.
mGluR6
The glutamate receptor of ON bipolar cells. It inverts the receptor signal and is found almost only in the retina.
