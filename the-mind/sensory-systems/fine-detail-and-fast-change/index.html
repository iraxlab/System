Fine Detail and Fast Change

What it is. Two parallel systems run from retina to cortex. The midget system carries fine detail, texture, colour and fine depth. The parasol system carries fast flicker and fast, faint motion.

What it gives you. It explains why camouflage fails the moment an animal moves, and why the edge of vision is good at catching movement but poor at reading.

Two systems, two jobs

Midget ganglion cells have small fields and a sustained response. Parasol cells have fields about three times wider and fire a brief burst. In the fovea midget cells outnumber parasol cells eight to one; in the periphery the two are about equal.

To learn what each system does, small areas of either the small-cell or the large-cell layers of the thalamus were silenced in monkeys. The animals were then tested on many visual tasks, with targets placed in the affected or the healthy part of the visual field.

The result is a clear division of labour. Neither system is enough alone, and many tasks use both. The two exist because one cell cannot be both sustained and brief, or both finely detailed and highly sensitive.

Lost when the small-cell layers are silenced

  • Colour Colour discrimination disappears completely.
  • Fine detail Sensitivity to fine stripes drops sharply.
  • Texture A patch of differently tilted lines can no longer be picked out.
  • Pattern Small differences between checkerboards are missed.
  • Fine depth Small differences between the two eyes' images are no longer seen as depth.
  • Shape Fine form suffers; coarse form survives.

Lost when the large-cell layers are silenced

  • Fast flicker Rapid flicker is badly affected.
  • Fast motion Faint, fast-moving dots are much harder to see.
  • Nothing else much Colour, detail and depth stay normal.

Handled by both

  • Brightness Telling two greys apart survives either loss, in daylight and at night.
  • Coarse form and depth Large shapes and large depth differences use both systems.
  • Night vision Rods feed both systems, not only the parasol system as once claimed.

Where the two systems go

  • MT Silenced when the parasol layers are blocked. It leads on to the parietal lobe and motion.
  • V4 Reduced by blocking either system. Its input is mixed, not purely midget.
  • V1 Some cells receive one system, others receive both.

A random-dot stereogram shows no shape to either eye alone. Only when the brain compares the two images does a square float above the background.

Safety. Never look at the sun or a bright lamp, and stop if your eyes strain or water.

Steps

  1. Look straight ahead and hold a page of small print out to one side.
  2. You see that something is there but cannot read it. Midget cells, needed for detail, thin out away from the centre.
  3. Keep your gaze ahead and wiggle the page.
  4. The movement jumps out at once. Parasol cells are relatively common in the periphery.
  5. Look at a fluorescent tube or old screen out of the corner of your eye.
  6. Flicker is often easier to see off-centre, where the parasol system carries more weight.

Time. 3 minutes

Words to know

Parvocellular
The small-cell layers of the thalamus that relay the midget system.
Magnocellular
The large-cell layers of the thalamus that relay the parasol system.
Stereopsis
Depth seen from the small difference between the two eyes' images.
Spatial frequency
How fine a pattern is: many narrow stripes per degree is high, a few wide ones is low.
