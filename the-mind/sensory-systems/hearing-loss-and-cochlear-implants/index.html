Hearing Loss and Cochlear Implants

What it is. Most permanent hearing loss comes from damaged hair cells, usually through noise, drugs, ageing, genes or disease. Hearing aids amplify; cochlear implants bypass the hair cells and stimulate the nerve.

What it gives you. Noise damage is cumulative and preventable. The cochlear implant is the most successful restoration of a human sense by medicine, used by hundreds of thousands of people.

Why hearing fails

Conductive loss affects the eardrum or bones and can often be repaired. Sensorineural loss affects the cochlea or nerve; in about 99% of cases the hair cells are lost. Outer hair cells and the high-frequency base of the cochlea are the most vulnerable.

A typical pattern is normal hearing to about 1 kHz and a sloping loss above. Vowels sit at low frequencies but consonants such as "s" and "t" are high, so speech becomes muffled and hard to follow, especially in noise.

In one study of a right-handed hunter, the ear nearer the gun had lost almost all its high-frequency hair cells, while the other, shielded by the head, was largely intact. The nerve fibres survived, which is what makes implants possible.

Five main causes

  • Noise Gunshots and fireworks above 120 dB can damage in one exposure. Damage adds up over years.
  • Drugs Some antibiotics in the aminoglycoside family kill hair cells.
  • Ageing High frequencies fade with age. The mechanism is still being studied.
  • Genes Inherited deafness is common enough that newborn screening is standard in many places.
  • Disease Meningitis and other infections can destroy hearing.
  • Variation People differ greatly in how much noise damages them.

Aids and implants

  • Hearing aid Microphone, amplifier shaped to the loss, and a tiny speaker. Excellent for conductive loss, limited when tuning is broad.
  • Cochlear implant Sound is split into frequency bands and sent as pulses to about 22 electrodes along the cochlea, low pitches deep inside.
  • Brainstem and midbrain implants For people without a working nerve. Results are much weaker than with cochlear implants.

What users report

  1. At switch-on, voices sound high and cartoon-like. Within one to two months the brain adapts.
  2. Many users understand most words without lip-reading in quiet rooms.
  3. Noise, music and tonal languages stay hard, because implants give place but little timing information.
  4. The device is programmed yearly in a sound booth, matching loudness and pitch for each electrode.
  5. Children born deaf are implanted early, around 18 months, to use the critical period for language.

Implants are taken off for sleep, showering and swimming, so visual or vibrating alarms are used at night.

Hearing loss and implants

  • 85 dB over 8 hours: protection required at work in the United States
  • 120 dB+ can damage in a single exposure
  • 20 dB reduction from a well-fitted foam earplug
  • 22 electrode contacts on a common implant array
  • 18 months typical age for implanting a child born deaf
  • 320,000+ implant users worldwide by 2013

Bird hair cells regrow. The discovery came by chance, when researchers returned from a three-week break and found that damaged hair cells had come back.

Safety. Seek a hearing test after sudden hearing loss, ear pain or lasting ringing. Never use earplugs in place of medical care.

Steps

  1. Check the noise level of a place you visit often with a sound meter app.
  2. Above 85 dB for long periods damages hearing. Concerts, power tools and clubs often exceed 100 dB.
  3. Roll a foam earplug thin, insert it, and hold it while it expands.
  4. A good fit cuts about 20 dB. Sounds are softer but still clear.
  5. After a loud event, notice any ringing or muffled hearing.
  6. Ringing means the inner ear was stressed. Rest your ears and protect them next time.

Time. 5 minutes

Words to know

Sensorineural loss
Hearing loss from the cochlea or nerve, most often lost hair cells.
Audiogram
A chart of the quietest sound heard at each frequency, compared with normal hearing.
Ototoxic
Harmful to the inner ear, as some antibiotics are.
Critical period
An early window when the brain learns a skill such as language most easily.
