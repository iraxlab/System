Welcome to Neuroscience

What it is. Neuroscience studies the nervous system at every scale, from single molecules to conscious thought, around one central question: how does physical tissue produce a mind?

What it gives you. It sets out the map for the whole part and shows how the idea that the brain, not the heart, governs behaviour was argued over for thousands of years before it was proven.

What this field covers

Neuroscience is a young name for an old question. The Society for Neuroscience was founded in 1970, but people have argued about where thought lives for at least seven thousand years. Today the field joins biology, chemistry, physics, medicine and psychology.

The work moves across scales. A single ion channel opening in a membrane, a circuit in the spinal cord and a decision made in the frontal lobe are all part of one system. Each level explains the one above it and is limited by the one below.

This part follows the same order as the course: first the single cell, then the major systems for sensing and moving, and finally behaviour, including memory, attention, emotion, sleep and mental illness.

Three levels, in order

  • The cell Topics 2 to 6 cover how one neuron holds an electrical charge, fires a signal and passes it to the next cell through chemistry.
  • The systems Topics 7 to 14 cover the layout of the brain, then vision, hearing, smell, taste and the control of movement.
  • Behaviour Topics 15 to 22 cover brain chemistry, mental illness, motivation, memory, attention, emotion and sleep.

Each level rests on the one before it, so the order matters.

Seven thousand years of argument

  1. Prehistoric skulls show deliberate holes drilled in living people. Healed bone proves many survived the procedure.
  2. Ancient Egyptian physicians placed the mind in the heart. The brain was removed through the nose before burial and discarded.
  3. Hippocrates argued that the brain is the organ of feeling and thought. Aristotle disagreed and saw it as a radiator that cools the blood.
  4. Galen, physician to Roman gladiators, studied sheep brains and described fluid-filled ventricles. His model held for 1,500 years.
  5. Renaissance anatomists turned to the tissue itself and separated grey matter, rich in cells, from white matter, made of fibres.

The correct view existed early. It took evidence, not authority, to make it stick.

Six findings that built the modern field

  • Nerves are wires Electrical stimulation makes muscles twitch, and the brain itself produces electrical current. Nerves conduct signals, not fluid.
  • One-way traffic In each spinal nerve, the rear root carries sensation in and the front root carries commands out to the muscles.
  • Bumps mean nothing Phrenology claimed character could be read from skull shape. It was popular, untested and wrong.
  • Place predicts function A patient who lost speech but kept comprehension had damage in one area of the left frontal lobe, now called Broca's area.
  • Shared ancestry Darwin noted that fear and anger look alike across mammals. This is why animal research applies to human brains.
  • The neuron By 1900, staining methods showed the nervous system is built from separate cells, each a discrete unit.

From molecule to mind

  • Molecular The proteins, transmitters and genes that build neurons and carry their messages.
  • Cellular How a single neuron receives input, makes a decision and sends output to other cells.
  • Systems Circuits of neurons that work together for one purpose, such as seeing or walking.
  • Behavioural How systems combine to produce memory, mood, language and dreaming.
  • Cognitive How neural activity gives rise to self-awareness, mental imagery and abstract thought.
  • The method Observe, replicate, interpret and submit to review. Independent checking is what catches error and fraud.

The cost of the unknown (US figures, mid-2000s)

  • 50% of people over 85 live with some form of dementia
  • 1.5 million Americans live with Parkinson's disease
  • 33 million Americans will have major depression at some point
  • 2 million Americans live with schizophrenia
  • 3rd place for stroke among leading causes of death
  • 1970 the Society for Neuroscience is founded

More hospital beds are filled by neurological and psychiatric conditions than by any other category of illness, including heart disease and cancer.

Steps

  1. Sit upright, close your eyes and rest both hands on your knees.
  2. Bring one index finger to the tip of your nose without looking.
  3. Stretch receptors in your muscles and joints reported the arm's position. This sense is called proprioception.
  4. Name three separate sounds in the room.
  5. Each sound passed from the ear through the brainstem and thalamus to the cortex in well under a second.
  6. Place one hand on your forehead and the other on the back of your head.
  7. Behind the forehead sits planning and judgement; at the back sits vision. Function follows location.

Time. 3 minutes

Words to know

Neuron
A nerve cell: the cell type that receives, combines and sends electrical signals.
Localization of function
The principle that different regions of the brain handle different tasks.
Levels of analysis
The scales at which the brain is studied: molecular, cellular, systems, behavioural and cognitive.
