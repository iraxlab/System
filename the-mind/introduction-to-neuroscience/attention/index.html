Attention

What it is. Attention is the selective processing of some information over the rest. It enhances neural responses to what we attend to and suppresses what we ignore.

What it gives you. Attention shapes what we perceive and remember. Its disorders, from ADHD to neglect after stroke, show how much ordinary experience depends on it.

Choosing what to process

The senses deliver far more information than the brain can fully process. Attention selects. At a crowded party, it lets you follow one conversation and ignore the rest, yet still catch your name spoken across the room.

Attention can be directed by choice, such as searching for a friend in a crowd, or captured by events, such as a sudden noise. It can focus on a location, a feature such as colour, or a whole object.

In the brain, attention increases the response of neurons to attended stimuli and shrinks their response to distractors. The effect is measurable from early visual cortex onward.

What attention does for performance

  • Better detection Faint stimuli at an attended location are detected more often than identical stimuli elsewhere.
  • Faster reactions A cue pointing to where a target will appear speeds responses by tens of milliseconds.
  • Cost of distraction A misleading cue slows responses, showing that attention has to move.

When half the world disappears

  1. Damage to the right parietal lobe, often from stroke, can cause neglect of the left side of space.
  2. Patients may eat only the right half of their plate or shave only one side of the face.
  3. Asked to copy a drawing, they reproduce only its right half.
  4. Their eyes work normally. The problem is in attending to that side.
  5. Neglect often improves over weeks to months as the brain recovers.

The right hemisphere appears to attend to both sides; the left mainly to the right.

What neurons do

  • fMRI Attending to one location increases activity in the matching part of the visual cortex.
  • PET Attending to colour or motion raises activity in the areas specialised for each.
  • Parietal cortex Neurons respond more strongly to a stimulus when it is the target of attention.
  • Area V4 Receptive fields shrink around an attended object, filtering out distractors.
  • Pulvinar This thalamic nucleus links many visual areas and helps coordinate where attention goes.
  • Eye movements Areas that plan eye movements also direct attention. Planning a look shifts attention first.

Attention-deficit hyperactivity disorder

  • Features Inattention, hyperactivity and impulsiveness beyond what is typical for age, affecting school, work and relationships.
  • Biology Strongly heritable. Linked to differences in dopamine and norepinephrine systems and in frontal-striatal circuits.
  • Treatment Stimulants such as methylphenidate raise dopamine and norepinephrine and improve focus for many people.

Attention

  • 5-8% of children are diagnosed with ADHD
  • 30-50 ms faster reactions with a valid cue
  • right hemisphere damage most often causes neglect
  • 1953 the cocktail party effect described
  • 3 targets: location, feature, object
  • 50% of children with ADHD still have symptoms as adults

In change-blindness experiments, people often fail to notice large changes in a scene, such as a different person taking over a conversation, when their attention is elsewhere.

Steps

  1. Look at a point straight ahead and keep your eyes still.
  2. Without moving your eyes, shift your attention to an object at the edge of your vision.
  3. You perceive it more clearly. Attention can move independently of gaze.
  4. In a noisy room, focus on one conversation.
  5. Other voices fade, but your own name in another conversation will still catch your attention.
  6. Read a paragraph while music plays.
  7. You notice less of the music. Attention to one sense reduces processing in another.

Time. 3 minutes

Words to know

Selective attention
Focusing processing on one source of information while ignoring others.
Neglect syndrome
After damage, usually to the right parietal lobe, a person ignores one side of space.
Pulvinar
A large thalamic nucleus thought to help direct attention across the cortex.
