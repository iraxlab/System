Smell and Taste

What it is. Taste and smell are chemical senses. Taste detects five basic qualities on the tongue; smell detects thousands of airborne molecules in the nose. Together they create flavour.

What it gives you. These are the oldest senses. They protect against spoiled food and toxins, shape appetite and connect directly to memory and emotion.

The oldest senses

Even bacteria swim toward food and away from poisons by detecting chemicals. In humans, chemical sensing has expanded into two separate systems. Taste tells us whether something in the mouth is safe and nourishing; smell samples the air for distant information.

Taste has five basic qualities: salty, sour, sweet, bitter and umami, the savoury taste of amino acids like glutamate. Each is sensed by cells in taste buds on the tongue, palate and throat. The idea of a tongue map with separate zones is a myth.

Smell is far richer. Humans have about 350 working odorant receptor types and can tell apart thousands of odours. Each odour activates a unique combination of receptors, read by the brain like a chord.

Five qualities, five mechanisms

  • Salty Sodium enters taste cells directly through dedicated sodium channels.
  • Sour Acids release hydrogen ions, which change the cell's charge.
  • Bitter About 30 receptor types detect a wide range of plant toxins. Sensitivity is very high.
  • Sweet One receptor made of two subunits detects sugars and artificial sweeteners.
  • Umami A related receptor detects glutamate, found in meat, cheese and tomatoes.
  • Taste buds Each holds 50 to 150 receptor cells. They are replaced about every two weeks.

From tongue to cortex

  1. Three cranial nerves carry taste from different parts of the mouth.
  2. They converge on the gustatory nucleus in the medulla.
  3. Signals pass to the thalamus, then to primary gustatory cortex near the insula.
  4. Branches also reach the hypothalamus and amygdala, linking taste to appetite and emotion.
  5. Each taste cell is tuned to one quality, and its path to the brain is largely separate.

A single bad experience with a food can create a lasting aversion, even when the cause was something else.

How odours are detected

  • Receptor neurons About 10 million per nose, in a patch of tissue high in the nasal cavity. They are replaced every four to eight weeks.
  • Receptor genes Mice have about 1,000 odorant receptor genes, humans about 350 functional ones. Each neuron expresses only one.
  • Transduction An odorant binds its receptor, triggering a G-protein cascade that opens channels and fires the neuron.

Buck and Axel discovered the odorant receptor family and shared the Nobel Prize in 2004.

A direct line to memory

  1. Receptor axons pass through small holes in the skull to the olfactory bulb.
  2. All neurons with the same receptor converge on one or two glomeruli, about 2,000 in each bulb.
  3. Each odour creates a distinct spatial pattern across the glomeruli.
  4. Bulb output reaches olfactory cortex, the amygdala and areas near the hippocampus.
  5. Some routes bypass the thalamus, unlike every other sense.

This direct link to emotion and memory explains why smells can recall events so vividly.

The chemical senses

  • 5 basic taste qualities
  • 2,000-5,000 taste buds in the mouth
  • 350 working odorant receptor types in humans
  • 10 million olfactory receptor neurons in the nose
  • 2 weeks lifespan of a taste receptor cell
  • 2004 Nobel Prize for odorant receptors

Whether humans respond to pheromones is still debated. Some studies found that women living together tend to synchronise their cycles, but later studies have struggled to repeat the finding.

Safety. Use only foods you know are safe for you.

Steps

  1. Take a jelly bean or a piece of fruit and pinch your nose shut.
  2. Chew slowly and note what you taste.
  3. You detect sweetness and sourness but not the specific flavour. That comes from smell.
  4. Release your nose while still chewing.
  5. Flavour arrives at once, as aroma travels from the back of the mouth up to the nose.
  6. Smell a familiar scent from childhood.
  7. Smell signals reach memory and emotion areas without first passing through the thalamus.

Time. 2 minutes

Words to know

Gustation
The sense of taste.
Olfaction
The sense of smell.
Glomerulus
A small cluster in the olfactory bulb where all receptor cells of one type converge.
