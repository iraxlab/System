When the Brain Is Unwell

What it is. Mental illnesses are disorders of brain function influenced by genes, development and experience. Anxiety disorders, mood disorders and schizophrenia are the most studied, and each has effective treatments.

What it gives you. Seeing mental illness as a brain condition reduces stigma, guides treatment and explains why medication and therapy both work.

Illness of the brain, not of character

For much of history, mental illness was blamed on spirits, weak will or poor upbringing. Modern evidence shows that it arises from the same kinds of processes as other brain conditions: genes, development, chemistry and experience acting together.

Two approaches complement each other. The psychosocial approach looks at life history, thought patterns and relationships. The biological approach looks at genes, circuits and transmitters. Both are needed for a full picture.

No single gene causes these disorders. Many genes each add a small amount of risk. Whether illness develops often depends on stress, timing and environment.

Fear out of proportion

  • Panic disorder Sudden attacks of intense fear with a pounding heart, breathlessness and a sense of doom, often without a trigger.
  • Agoraphobia Fear of places where escape is hard. It often develops after panic attacks and can confine people to their homes.
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder Intrusive thoughts and repeated rituals to relieve them, linked to overactive frontal-basal ganglia loops.

Where anxiety comes from

  1. The amygdala detects threat and activates the hypothalamus.
  2. The hypothalamus triggers cortisol release through the pituitary and adrenal glands.
  3. The hippocampus normally senses cortisol and switches the response off.
  4. Chronic stress weakens hippocampal control and strengthens the amygdala.
  5. The result is a stress system stuck in the on position.

Benzodiazepines calm this circuit by strengthening GABA inhibition; SSRIs also reduce anxiety over weeks.

Depression and bipolar disorder

  • Major depression Low mood, loss of pleasure, poor sleep, guilt and fatigue for at least two weeks. Affects about one in five people over a lifetime.
  • Bipolar disorder Episodes of depression alternate with mania: elevated mood, little sleep, racing thoughts and risky decisions.
  • Monoamine theory Many antidepressants raise norepinephrine or serotonin, suggesting a role for these systems.
  • The delay Antidepressants raise transmitter levels within hours, but mood improves only after weeks of slower brain changes.
  • Lithium A simple salt that stabilises mood in bipolar disorder. Its exact mechanism is still being studied.
  • ECT Electroconvulsive therapy, given under anaesthesia, remains one of the most effective treatments for severe depression.

A disorder of perception and thought

  • Symptoms Hallucinations, delusions and disorganised thinking, along with reduced motivation and emotional expression.
  • Dopamine hypothesis Drugs that block dopamine D2 receptors reduce psychosis; drugs that raise dopamine can produce it.
  • Glutamate hypothesis Drugs that block NMDA glutamate receptors, such as PCP, mimic the full range of symptoms.

Onset is usually in late teens or early twenties. Genes and early environment both raise risk.

Mental illness

  • 1 in 5 adults have a mental illness in a given year
  • 1% of people develop schizophrenia
  • 50% concordance for schizophrenia in identical twins
  • 2-6 weeks before antidepressants improve mood
  • 1949 lithium first reported to treat mania
  • 1950s first antipsychotics and antidepressants introduced

The first antidepressants were found by chance. A drug developed for tuberculosis in the 1950s left patients unexpectedly cheerful, which led researchers to the monoamine systems.

Safety. This topic is educational. For any mental health concern, speak with a doctor or qualified therapist.

Steps

  1. Pick one condition, such as depression.
  2. Ask what it looks like in behaviour and experience.
  3. Persistent low mood, loss of interest, poor sleep and appetite, lasting at least two weeks.
  4. Ask what is known about its biology.
  5. Genes raise vulnerability. Stress and a dysregulated cortisol response often act as triggers.
  6. Ask how it is treated.
  7. Psychotherapy, medication, or both. Each changes brain function, and they work best together.

Time. 10 minutes a day

Words to know

Anxiolytic
A medication that reduces anxiety, such as a benzodiazepine.
Diathesis-stress model
The idea that illness arises from an inherited vulnerability combined with environmental stress.
Antipsychotic
A medication that reduces psychotic symptoms, mostly by blocking dopamine D2 receptors.
